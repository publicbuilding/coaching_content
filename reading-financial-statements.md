# Reading Financial Statements in 10 Minutes

## Target Audience
- Executive leaders without accounting background
- Business owners making quick decisions
- Managers needing financial literacy

## Document Length
- **Target:** 4-5 pages
- **Read Time:** 10 minutes

---

## I. The Three Core Statements (Overview)
**Length:** 0.5 page | **Time:** 1 minute

### Key Points
- Income Statement: What did we make/spend?
- Balance Sheet: What do we own/owe?
- Cash Flow: Where did money go?
- Why all three matter (profitability ≠ cash health)

### Why It Matters
- Each tells a different story
- Together they reveal financial health
- One missing piece creates blind spots

---

## II. The Income Statement (The Profit Machine)
**Length:** 1.5 pages | **Time:** 3 minutes

### Key Sections
1. **Revenue** (Top line)
   - Total sales/income
   - Growth indicators
   - Revenue quality (recurring vs. one-time)

2. **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)**
   - Direct production costs
   - Gross margin calculation
   - Industry benchmarks

3. **Operating Expenses**
   - SG&A (Sales, General, Admin)
   - R&D spending
   - Overhead trends

4. **EBITDA & Operating Income**
   - Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation
   - Why it matters: shows operational performance
   - Better than net income for comparisons

5. **Bottom Line (Net Income)**
   - What actually stays with the company
   - Earnings per share (if public)
   - Margin analysis (what % of revenue is profit?)

### Critical Metrics to Look For
- Revenue growth year-over-year
- Gross margin stability
- Operating margin trends
- Expense ratios (OPEX as % of revenue)

### Red Flags
- Revenue up but profit down (efficiency problem)
- Wildly inconsistent EBITDA
- Expenses growing faster than revenue

---

## III. The Balance Sheet (The Snapshot)
**Length:** 1.5 pages | **Time:** 3 minutes

### Assets (What We Own)
1. **Current Assets** (convertible to cash within 1 year)
   - Cash & cash equivalents
   - Accounts receivable (money customers owe)
   - Inventory
   - Importance: liquidity

2. **Fixed Assets** (long-term)
   - Property, equipment, buildings
   - Intangible assets (patents, goodwill)
   - Depreciation impact

### Liabilities (What We Owe)
1. **Current Liabilities** (due within 1 year)
   - Accounts payable
   - Short-term debt
   - Accrued expenses

2. **Long-Term Liabilities**
   - Bonds, mortgages
   - Pension obligations
   - Why it matters: solvency risk

### Equity (Ownership Stake)
- Assets - Liabilities = Equity
- Retained earnings vs. shareholder equity
- Importance: financial stability

### Key Ratios
- **Current Ratio:** Current Assets / Current Liabilities
  - Above 1.5 = healthy
  - Below 1.0 = cash crisis risk
- **Debt-to-Equity:** Total Debt / Equity
  - Lower is safer
  - Industry dependent

### Red Flags
- Current ratio below 1.0
- Rapidly growing debt
- Inventory growing faster than sales
- Accounts receivable aging (customers not paying)

---

## IV. Cash Flow Statement (The Reality Check)
**Length:** 1 page | **Time:** 2 minutes

### Three Sections
1. **Operating Cash Flow**
   - Cash generated from business operations
   - Most important number
   - Should be positive and growing

2. **Investing Cash Flow**
   - Capital expenditures (buying equipment, etc.)
   - Acquisitions
   - Expected to be negative (investment)

3. **Financing Cash Flow**
   - Debt raised/repaid
   - Equity issued/bought back
   - Dividends paid

### Why It Matters
- Profitable ≠ cash positive
- Cash flow delays cause bankruptcy
- Operating cash flow is the truth

### Red Flags
- Negative operating cash flow
- Cash flow declining while profit rises
- Burning cash for operations

---

## V. Quick Reading Checklist
**Length:** 0.5 page | **Time:** 1 minute

### In 60 Seconds:
- [ ] Revenue growing?
- [ ] Profit growing faster/same rate?
- [ ] Operating cash flow positive?
- [ ] Current ratio above 1.5?
- [ ] Debt manageable (debt-to-equity reasonable)?

### Red Flag Summary
- Profit but no cash = problem
- Growing expenses + flat revenue = problem
- Growing inventory + flat sales = problem
- Negative current ratio = problem

## PAGES 3-4: WHY YOUR FIRST 90 DAYS MATTER

**The Truth Most New Executives Don't Know**

Your first 90 days aren't about proving yourself competent. People already believe that—you wouldn't have been hired otherwise.

Your first 90 days are about establishing three things:

1. **You understand the culture** (not intellectually—viscerally)
2. **You earn trust** (through listening, follow-through, and quick wins)
3. **You've figured out what actually matters** (vs. what people say matters)

Get these three things right, and you have leverage for everything that comes next. Get them wrong, and you're fighting credibility battles for your entire tenure.

**Why Most New Executives Fail**

They move too fast.

They think momentum = speed. They want to prove they're taking action, so they make changes before they understand the system. They implement their playbook from their last role without asking if it fits.

Then they wonder why people resist.

**What Actually Works**

The executives who move mountains in their first year usually don't make dramatic moves in their first 90 days. They listen more than they talk. They ask more than they answer. They build relationships before they build initiatives.

This is counterintuitive. It feels like wasting time. But it's actually the fastest path to real influence.

This playbook is based on conversations with 50+ executives who've transitioned into new roles and dramatically increased their impact. Not the theoretical "best practices." The actual moves that worked when the stakes were real.

## PAGES 5-7: THE 30-60-90 FRAMEWORK

**A Roadmap for Your First Quarter**

### DAYS 1-30: LISTEN, LEARN, BUILD RELATIONSHIPS

**The Core Objective:** Understand the system without judgment. Build trust with key people. Show you're willing to learn.

**What to do:**

**1-on-1 meetings (Your first priority)**
- Schedule 1-on-1s with direct reports in your first week
- Meet your boss's peers
- Talk to people 2-3 levels below you (skip levels matter)
- What to ask: "Help me understand how things actually work. What's working well? What's broken? What are people afraid to say in public?"

**Listen for patterns**
- What problems show up in multiple conversations?
- Where is the organization frustrated with previous leaders?
- What's the actual culture vs. the stated culture?
- Who has influence (formal and informal)?

**Make zero changes**
- This is hard. Resist it.
- You'll see things you want to fix. Write them down. Don't fix them yet.
- Moving too fast signals: "I think I'm smarter than you" and "I'm not willing to understand your context."

**One small win**
- Not a massive initiative. One thing that's visibly broken.
- Ask people first: "If I could fix one thing that would make your job better, what would it be?"
- Fix it in the first 30 days. Make it visible.
- This shows: "I listen, I act, I follow through."

**Establish rhythm**
- Regular all-hands meetings
- Office hours (open door for questions)
- Weekly 1:1s with direct reports
- Predictable communication

**Days 1-30 Success Looks Like:**
- People feel heard
- You've built 5-10 key relationships
- One small win is visible
- You haven't made any major enemies
- You've asked more questions than you've answered

---

### DAYS 31-60: ESTABLISH AUTHORITY & CREATE MOMENTUM

**The Core Objective:** Show you understand the business. Deliver visible results. Position yourself as a problem-solver.

**What to do:**

**Demonstrate competence fast**
- Find one area where you have clear expertise
- Solve a problem or improve a process in this area
- Make it visible (share what you did and why)
- This establishes: "This person knows what they're talking about"

**Make your first strategic move**
- Based on your listening, identify one thing that's strategically wrong
- Propose a change (but frame it as collaborative, not directive)
- "I've noticed [problem]. Here's how I'm thinking about it. What am I missing?"
- Implement it with buy-in, not by mandate

**Hire your first key person (if applicable)**
- If you have hiring authority, this is your leverage point
- Hire someone who fills a gap you've identified
- Make sure the org knows this person solves a real problem
- Good hires create momentum

**Build your leadership bench**
- Identify 2-3 people who have potential to grow
- Start investing in them (1:1s, opportunities, stretch assignments)
- This signals: "I believe in this team" and "I'm building culture"

**Start small initiatives**
- Not big transformation yet
- Multiple small projects that show momentum
- Each one should demonstrate: listening, follow-through, competence, and care for the team

**Days 31-60 Success Looks Like:**
- One strategic move implemented with buy-in
- Visible momentum (projects moving, people energized)
- You've established expertise in at least one area
- Your team sees you making good decisions
- Buy-in is building instead of resistance

### DAYS 61-90: SET STRATEGIC DIRECTION

**The Core Objective:** Articulate where you're taking the organization. Rally people around that direction.

**What to do:**

**Articulate your vision**
- Based on 60 days of listening, what do you believe the org should become?
- Not generic ("grow revenue, improve culture")
- Specific, ambitious, and grounded in real opportunity
- "We're going to become [specific thing] because [why it matters]"

**Frame it around the people, not you**
- "I believe this team can become X" (not "I'm going to make us X")
- Show how this is their success, not your proof point
- Connect it to their values and ambitions

**Create first quarter plan**
- What are the 3-5 initiatives that move toward this vision?
- How do they connect to the vision?
- What's the sequence (what comes first and why)?
- Who owns what?

**Establish decision-making clarity**
- How do people get decisions made?
- What matters (decisions you'll make) vs. what doesn't (people can decide)
- Create a decision framework people can understand

**Start your leadership team**
- Regular meetings with your key leaders
- Same rhythm, same people
- Focused on strategy, not logistics

**Days 61-90 Success Looks Like:**
- You've articulated a clear vision
- Multiple people are excited about where you're going
- You have a plan and people understand it
- Key decisions are clear
- You've established yourself as competent, trustworthy, and thinking strategically

## PAGES 8-11: THE CORE PRINCIPLES THAT CHANGE EVERYTHING

**Principle 1: Listen More Than You Talk**

Your first instinct will be to prove you know what you're doing. Resist it.

Listening is power. When you ask good questions, people think you're smart. When you answer too many questions, people wonder if you actually get it.

For every answer you give in your first 90 days, give at least three thoughtful questions.

**Principle 2: Relationships Before Initiatives**

This is backwards from how most people think about power.

We think: Get authority → make changes → gain respect.

Actually it's: Build relationships → earn trust → get buy-in → make changes → cement respect.

The relationship-building phase feels slow. It's not. It's the difference between people following you and people resisting you.

**Principle 3: Make Promises You Can Keep**

Your credibility is your currency.

In your first 90 days, underpromise and overdeliver. Say "I'll get back to you" more than you say "yes." Follow up on every commitment, even small ones.

One broken promise early destroys momentum. Ten kept promises build it.

**Principle 4: Admit What You Don't Know**

Your team already knows what you don't know. Pretending certainty signals that you don't respect their intelligence.

"I don't have experience with X, which is why I want to understand how you approach it" is far more powerful than pretending.

**Principle 5: Make One Thing Visible**

In your first 60 days, you need one clear win.

Not massive. Not transformational. Just one thing that proves: "This person listens, acts, and delivers."

This gives people permission to trust you on bigger things later.

**Principle 6: Protect Your Team**

Make decisions that protect your people, even if it costs you politically.

This is the fastest way to earn loyalty. People follow leaders who have their back.

**Principle 7: Be Consistent, Not Perfect**

You don't need to be brilliant in your first 90 days. You need to be consistent.

Same tone, same values, same follow-through, same vision. Consistency is what builds credibility.

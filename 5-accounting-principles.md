## II. Principle #1: The Accrual Principle
**Length:** 0.8 pages | **Time:** 2 minutes

### The Core Idea
- Revenue recognized when earned, not when cash received
- Expenses recognized when incurred, not when paid
- Creates "true" profit picture (not just cash)

### Why It Matters for Leaders
- Sales in December might show up next quarter (accrual timing)
- A profitable company can go bankrupt (cash flow mismatch)
- Accrual accounting prevents misleading profit claims

### Business Examples
1. **SaaS Company:**
   - Sign 3-year contract in January
   - Under accrual: recognize full revenue immediately
   - Under cash: only recognize monthly payments received
   - Executive decision: pricing model affects when profit is recognized

2. **Manufacturing:**
   - Orders components in Nov, install in Jan
   - Expense recognized Nov (when incurred)
   - Revenue recognized Jan (when delivered)
   - Executive decision: inventory decisions affect profit timing

### Red Flags
- Revenue spiked but accounts receivable are massive (cash collection issue)
- Profit up but accounts payable also up (expense timing manipulation)
- Gap between GAAP profit and operating cash flow expanding

### Leader Implication
"Growth on paper ≠ growth in bank account. Always watch cash."

## III. Principle #2: The Matching Principle
**Length:** 0.8 pages | **Time:** 2 minutes

### The Core Idea
- Revenue and related expenses matched in same period
- Depreciation & amortization spread costs over time
- Prevents distorting profit by timing expenses

### Why It Matters for Leaders
- A major decision's financial impact spreads over years
- You can't hide costs by deferring them
- Forces strategic thinking about long-term investments

### Business Examples
1. **Acquisition Costs:**
   - Buy company for $10M, record as asset
   - Amortize over 5 years = $2M/year expense
   - Shows true annual cost of integration

2. **Equipment Purchase:**
   - Buy $500K machinery
   - Depreciate over 10 years = $50K/year
   - Shows true cost of running the operation

3. **Sales Commission:**
   - Pay salesman 10% of sales they generate
   - Commission expense matched to revenue month
   - Prevents lumpy profit by timing payments

### Red Flags
- Huge one-time charges (asset write-downs)
- Frequent adjustments to depreciation schedules
- Excessive "goodwill" from acquisitions (harder to verify)

### Leader Implication
"Every decision has costs that play out over time. The matching principle forces transparency."

## IV. Principle #3: The Conservatism Principle
**Length:** 0.8 pages | **Time:** 2 minutes

### The Core Idea
- When in doubt, understate assets and profits
- Overstate liabilities and expenses
- Bias toward caution in financial reporting

### Why It Matters for Leaders
- Creates trust in financial statements (more conservative = more believable)
- Prevents over-optimistic projections
- Protects against external party manipulation

### Business Examples
1. **Warranty Reserves:**
   - You don't know exact warranty claims
   - Conservative approach: overestimate reserves
   - Protects from surprise costs

2. **Customer Discounts:**
   - Customer disputes invoice quality
   - Conservative: assume you'll lose some (take loss now)
   - Better than overstating revenue then reversing it

3. **Litigation:**
   - Company facing lawsuit
   - Conservative: accrue for likely loss now
   - Prevents earnings surprise if judgment comes

### Red Flags
- Unusually optimistic accounting estimates
- Constantly beating revenue guidance by large margins
- Slow to take write-downs on struggling assets

### Leader Implication
"Conservative accounting = trustworthy numbers. Aggressive accounting = watch out."

## V. Principle #4: The Consistency Principle
**Length:** 0.8 pages | **Time:** 2 minutes

### The Core Idea
- Use the same accounting methods year-to-year
- Changes require disclosure and restatement
- Ensures comparability over time

### Why It Matters for Leaders
- Year-over-year growth is meaningful (apples-to-apples)
- Accounting changes are red flags (possible manipulation)
- Prevents cheating by changing methods mid-stream

### Business Examples
1. **Inventory Accounting:**
   - FIFO (First In, First Out) vs. LIFO (Last In, First Out)
   - Different methods = different profit in inflation
   - Must stick with one method year-to-year

2. **Revenue Recognition:**
   - Recognizing at invoice vs. at shipment vs. at cash
   - Method must stay consistent
   - Change requires restatement of prior years

3. **Depreciation:**
   - Straight-line vs. accelerated
   - Must stay consistent
   - Changes heavily disclosed

### Red Flags
- Multiple accounting changes in short period
- Changes that improve reported earnings
- Inconsistency explanations that don't make sense

### Leader Implication
"If the accounting method changes, the numbers aren't comparable. Demand detailed explanation."

## VI. Principle #5: The Substance Over Form Principle
**Length:** 0.8 pages | **Time:** 2 minutes

### The Core Idea
- Account for the economic reality, not just legal structure
- A lease that's really a purchase is a purchase (under IFRS)
- Prevents companies from hiding true obligations

### Why It Matters for Leaders
- True liabilities must be reported (can't hide debt)
- Related-party transactions must be disclosed
- Special purpose vehicles can't hide risks

### Business Examples
1. **Operating Leases (New Rules):**
   - Used to hide debt off balance sheet
   - New accounting: all leases on balance sheet
   - Shows true obligations (rent = long-term liability)

2. **Related Party Transactions:**
   - CEO's brother sells equipment to company
   - Substance rule: market-rate test (is price fair?)
   - Prevents hidden transfers of value

3. **Structured Financing:**
   - Company "sells" receivables but retains risk
   - Substance: still a liability (company at risk)
   - Must be shown as debt, not cash

### Red Flags
- Complex special purpose entities
- Heavy related-party transactions
- Off-balance-sheet arrangements
- Aggressive interpretations of disclosure rules

### Leader Implication
"Read between the lines. The legal structure might hide economic reality."

## VII. How These 5 Principles Interconnect
**Length:** 0.5 pages | **Time:** 1 minute

### The Framework
1. **Accrual + Matching** = True profit picture
2. **Conservatism** = Trust that picture
3. **Consistency** = Compare year-to-year
4. **Substance Over Form** = Ensures reality, not tricks

### Why Leaders Need All 5
- Miss one, miss critical insights
- Each prevents a different type of deception
- Together: create trustworthy financial statements

## VIII. Practical Application: Leadership Decision Guide
**Length:** 0.5 pages | **Time:** 1 minute

### When You See This... Ask This:

| What You See | Which Principle | What to Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue up 50% | Accrual + Conservatism | Is it all cash, or are receivables growing? |
| Profit but negative cash | Accrual + Matching | Why the timing gap? |
| Accounting method changed | Consistency | Why change? How much did it impact results? |
| Off-balance-sheet arrangement | Substance Over Form | What's the real obligation? |
| Unusual one-time charges | Matching + Conservatism | Is this prudent or burying costs? |

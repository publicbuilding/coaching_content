## II. Metric #1: Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
**Length:** 1 page | **Time:** 2 minutes

### What It Is
**CAC = Total marketing spend / Number of new customers acquired**

### The Formula
```
CAC = (Marketing salary + advertising + tools + agency + events) / New customers
```

### Example
- Total marketing budget: $100K/year
- New customers acquired: 50
- CAC: $100K ÷ 50 = **$2,000 per customer**

### Why It Matters
- Shows how much you're spending to win each customer
- Reveals if marketing is cost-effective
- Tells you if business model is viable

### How to Calculate
1. **Define "new customer"** (not lead, not trial - actual paying customer)
2. **Track all marketing spend:**
   - Salaries (full team)
   - Advertising (all channels)
   - Tools/software (HubSpot, analytics, etc.)
   - Agencies, freelancers
   - Events, content, sponsorships
3. **Measure acquisitions accurately:**
   - Track source of each customer (which campaign?)
   - Use UTM codes, unique landing pages, promo codes
   - Database of "how did you hear about us?"
4. **Calculate by channel:**
   - CAC for email marketing
   - CAC for paid ads
   - CAC for organic/referral
   - **Identify which channels work best**

### Benchmarks
| Business Type | Good CAC | Concerning |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS (Annual) | < 1x ACV | > 2x ACV |
| E-commerce | < 20% of average order value | > 30% of AOV |
| B2B Services | < 50% of first year revenue | > 100% of first year |
| Subscription | < 6 months of subscription value | > 12 months |

### Red Flags
- CAC growing year-over-year (losing efficiency)
- CAC varies wildly by channel (some channels wasteful)
- CAC not being measured (blind spot)
- CAC exceeds customer lifetime value (unsustainable)

### How to Improve CAC
1. **Reduce spend:** Eliminate low-ROI channels
2. **Increase conversions:** Better targeting, messaging, landing pages
3. **Improve targeting:** Focus on profitable customer segments
4. **Referral leverage:** Incentivize customer referrals (much lower cost)
5. **Content strategy:** Organic reach reduces paid CAC

### Decision Impact
"If your CAC is $2,000 but customers only spend $1,500 with you, your business doesn't work. Fix it or change your model."

## III. Metric #2: Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)
**Length:** 1 page | **Time:** 2 minutes

### What It Is
**CLV = Total revenue from customer - Total cost to serve them**

Or more specifically:
**CLV = (Average customer revenue per year × Years as customer) - Cost to serve**

### The Formula
```
CLV = (Annual revenue per customer × Average customer lifespan) - Cost to serve

Cost to serve = (Support salary + product cost + operational overhead) per customer
```

### Example
**B2B SaaS:**
- Average annual revenue per customer: $10,000
- Average customer stays: 4 years
- Cost to serve: $1,000/year
- CLV = ($10,000 × 4) - ($1,000 × 4) = **$36,000**

**E-commerce:**
- Average order value: $100
- Average customer lifetime orders: 12
- Gross margin: 60%
- Cost to serve/support: $20/lifetime
- CLV = ($100 × 12 × 60%) - $20 = **$700**

### Why It Matters
- Shows long-term value of each customer
- Justifies higher CAC if CLV is strong
- Reveals if business is sustainable
- Guides product/service strategy

### How to Calculate
1. **Measure average customer lifespan:**
   - How long does average customer stay?
   - Use historical data (how many customers from 2020 still active?)
   - Project forward based on churn rate

2. **Revenue per customer:**
   - Annual contract value, or
   - Average order value × repeat purchases

3. **Cost to serve:**
   - Customer support salary (allocated)
   - Payment processing fees
   - Hosting/infrastructure (allocated)
   - Fulfillment/delivery costs
   - **Only direct costs** (not marketing, which is separate)

4. **Calculate by customer segment:**
   - Enterprise customers vs. SMB
   - Acquired through paid vs. organic
   - Different products/services
   - **Identify high-value segments**

### Benchmarks
| Business Type | Good CLV | Concerning |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS | > 3x annual revenue | < 2x annual revenue |
| Subscription (monthly) | > $1,000 | < $300 |
| E-commerce (repeat purchase) | > 10x average order | < 5x average order |
| Consulting | > $100K | < $50K |

### Red Flags
- CLV declining (customers not staying long)
- CLV less than CAC × 3 (not enough margin)
- High churn (customers leaving early)
- Cost to serve increasing over time
- Revenue per customer flat/declining

### How to Improve CLV
1. **Increase retention:** Reduce churn through product/service quality
2. **Increase frequency:** Upsell, cross-sell, encourage repeat purchases
3. **Increase price:** Premium pricing for loyal customers
4. **Reduce cost to serve:** Automation, self-service, efficiency
5. **Segment:** Focus on high-CLV customer groups

### Decision Impact
"A $300 CAC makes sense if CLV is $10,000. But if CLV is $1,000, you're in trouble."

## IV. Metric #3: Ratio - CLV to CAC (The Health Check)
**Length:** 0.8 pages | **Time:** 1 minute

### What It Is
**CLV:CAC Ratio = Total customer lifetime value / Customer acquisition cost**

### The Formula
```
Healthy Ratio = CLV ÷ CAC

Example:
- CLV: $36,000
- CAC: $2,000
- Ratio: 36,000 ÷ 2,000 = 18:1
```

### What the Ratios Mean

| Ratio | Business Health | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| < 1:1 | **Dead** | You lose money on every customer |
| 1:1 to 2:1 | **Broken** | Barely sustainable, need operational improvement |
| 2:1 to 3:1 | **Struggling** | Needs action (lower CAC or improve CLV) |
| 3:1 to 5:1 | **Healthy** | Sustainable, room for reinvestment |
| 5:1 to 10:1 | **Strong** | Good business, can grow aggressively |
| > 10:1 | **Exceptional** | Highly profitable, very defensible |

### Why This Ratio Matters
- Simplest single indicator of marketing ROI
- Tells you if model is sustainable
- Guides how much you can spend on growth
- Predicts profitability

### Example Scenarios

**Scenario A: Growing but Unproblematic**
- CAC: $5,000, CLV: $15,000
- Ratio: 3:1 (healthy)
- Verdict: Sustainable, can increase marketing spend

**Scenario B: Growing but Burning Cash**
- CAC: $5,000, CLV: $8,000
- Ratio: 1.6:1 (struggling)
- Action: Reduce CAC through better targeting, OR improve CLV through retention

**Scenario C: Mature, Highly Profitable**
- CAC: $2,000, CLV: $30,000
- Ratio: 15:1 (exceptional)
- Strategy: Can invest heavily in growth, market dominance

### How to Use This Ratio for Decisions
1. **Current state:** Calculate your ratio today
2. **Target state:** What should it be for your business?
3. **Gaps:** Where is the biggest opportunity?
   - Too high CAC? Improve targeting or channel mix
   - Too low CLV? Improve product, retention, pricing
4. **Track:** Monitor ratio monthly
   - Improving ratio = marketing getting better
   - Declining ratio = warning sign

├─ Overall: $24,000

- Update all 3 metrics monthly
- Compare to previous months (trend)
- Identify improving vs. declining channels
- Adjust spend based on performance

## VI. Common Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)
**Length:** 0.5 pages | **Time:** 1 minute

| Mistake | Impact | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Count "leads" as customers | CAC looks better, reality is worse | Only count paying customers |
| Ignore cost to serve | CLV inflated | Subtract all operating costs |
| Attribute campaigns wrong | Data unreliable | Use UTM codes, unique links |
| Calculate once/year | Miss trends | Track monthly, weekly if possible |
| Mix channels | Can't optimize | Calculate CAC/CLV per channel |
| Use "vanity metrics" as proof | Executives misled | Replace with these 3 metrics |

## INDIVIDUAL PRODUCT PRICING: $29.99

### Why This Price Point Works

**Against free:**
- Free = low perceived value. People don't apply free products.
- $29.99 signals quality. Creates "I paid for this, I should use it" motivation.
- Free also means unlimited tire-kickers. Buyers are committed.

**Against $99+:**
- Too high for impulse purchase ($29.99 is "why not?" price).
- $29.99 is under the "I need to ask boss" threshold.
- Low barrier = more volume = better word-of-mouth.

**Against $9.99:**
- Too cheap = signals low quality.
- Cheapness actually *hurts* conversion (paradox: expensive = trustworthy).
- $29.99 = "real product," $9.99 = "discount bin"

### Price Justification (What You Tell Customers)

**Frame 1: Time Investment**
"1 hour with a financial advisor: $200-500. This module: 5-10 hours of learning for $29.99."
- Emphasizes value density
- Makes price seem obviously cheap
- Drives purchasing psychology

**Frame 2: Mistake Avoidance**
"One financial mistake you avoid: $10K-100K. This costs $29.99."
- ROI perspective
- Addresses risk/reward
- Makes price feel irrelevant

**Frame 3: Cost Per Hour**
"$29.99 for 5-10 hours of learning = $3-6/hour vs. $200-500/hour for a consultant"
- Math is undeniable
- Frames as investment, not cost

**Frame 4: Comparison**
"Management book from Amazon: $20 (one-time read). This: $29.99 (study + apply + return to)."
- Positions as premium, not expensive
- Emphasizes re-usability

## AVOIDING COMMON PRICING MISTAKES

**❌ Don't offer payment plans**
- Creates friction on Gumroad
- Low price doesn't justify setup complexity
- Most customers can afford $29.99 upfront

**❌ Don't discount too much, too often**
- Trains customers to wait for sales
- Damages perceived value
- Keep discounts rare (2-3x/year max)

**❌ Don't price based on cost**
- You spent 100 hours = irrelevant
- Price based on value delivered ($10K-100K)
- Your cost is sunk

**❌ Don't have too many SKUs**
- Confuses customers
- Dilutes brand
- Stick to 5-7 offerings max

**❌ Don't raise prices on existing customers**
- Lock in grandfathered pricing for early buyers
- Good will > short-term revenue bump
- New customers pay higher price

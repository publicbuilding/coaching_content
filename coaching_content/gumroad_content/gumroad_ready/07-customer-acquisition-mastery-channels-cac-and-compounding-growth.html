Introduction: The Math of Growth {#introduction}

Here's the brutal truth: Most acquisition spending is wasted.

A typical SaaS company:

  • Spends $100K on marketing
  • Acquires 50 customers
  • CAC: $2,000 per customer
  • LTV: $3,000 per customer
  • Ratio: 1.5x

This looks profitable (LTV > CAC). But it's actually terrible because:

1. It doesn't account for payback period (how long until the customer funds themselves) 2. It doesn't account for cash flow (you pay CAC upfront, get LTV over 12+ months) 3. It's not scalable (to 10x growth, you'd need a 10x better channel or a 10x better product)

A better company:

  • Spends $100K on marketing
  • Acquires 200 customers
  • CAC: $500 per customer
  • LTV: $5,000 per customer
  • Ratio: 10x, payback: 2-3 months

The difference isn't better marketing. It's better unit economics.

What This Module Covers

1. Unit economics - What actually matters (LTV, CAC, payback, ratio)

2. Channels - How to evaluate which channel to invest in 3. Optimization - How to squeeze 3x more efficiency from a channel 4. Multi-channel - How to build multiple acquisition engines 5. Scaling - How to grow from $1M to $10M+ ARR

Unit Economics: CAC and LTV {#unit-economics}

Everything in customer acquisition comes down to two numbers:

CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost): How much do you spend to acquire one customer? LTV (Lifetime Value): How much profit does that customer generate?

Calculating CAC

CAC = (Sales & Marketing Spend) / (New Customers Acquired)

Example:

  • Marketing spend this month: $10,000
  • Sales salaries allocated: $15,000
  • Total acquisition spend: $25,000
  • Customers acquired: 20
  • CAC: $25,000 / 20 = $1,250 per customer
Important: Include all costs:
  • Advertising spend (ads, tools)
  • Team salaries (% allocated to customer acquisition)
  • Software and tools (CRM, marketing automation)
  • Events and content (if used for acquisition)

Don't include:

  • Customer success (that's retention, not acquisition)
  • Product development (that's product, not acquisition)
  • Admin overhead (indirect)

Calculating LTV

LTV = (Average Revenue per Customer) × (Customer Lifespan) - (Cost to Serve)

Example for SaaS:

  • Average monthly revenue per customer: $500
  • Average customer lifespan: 24 months (churn rate determines this)
  • Cost to serve (support, hosting, payment processing): $100/customer/month

LTV = ($500 × 24) - ($100/customer/month × 24 months)

LTV = $12,000 - $2,400 = $9,600

The CAC:LTV Ratio

Your profitability is determined by the ratio of CAC to LTV:

CAC:LTV Ratio = CAC / LTV
  • 1:3 ratio = Healthy (you make $3 for every $1 spent)
  • 1:5 ratio = Great (you make $5 for every $1 spent)
  • 1:1 ratio = Breaking even (not scalable)
  • 1:2 ratio = Barely profitable (hard to scale)
The industry standard: You should aim for at least 1:3.

Payback Period

CAC:LTV ratio is good but incomplete. You also need to know: How long until the customer pays back their acquisition cost?

Payback Period = CAC / (Monthly Profit per Customer)

Example:

  • CAC: $1,250
  • Monthly profit per customer: $400
  • Payback period: $1,250 / $400 = 3.1 months
Why this matters:
  • If payback is 12+ months, you'll run out of cash scaling
  • If payback is 3-4 months, you can recycle those profits to fuel growth
  • If payback is <2 months, you can grow very quickly

Why These Numbers Matter

Your CAC and LTV determine:

1. Profitability: Can you make money on each customer? 2. Scalability: Can you afford to grow fast? 3. Channel viability: Which channels are actually worthwhile? Example:

Two companies, both $2M ARR:

Company A:
  • CAC: $500
  • LTV: $2,000
  • Ratio: 1:4
  • Payback: 2 months
Company B:
  • CAC: $2,000
  • LTV: $3,000
  • Ratio: 1:1.5
  • Payback: 15 months

Company A can grow 10x faster because they don't run out of cash. Every customer pays for themselves in 2 months, letting them recycle profits into acquisition.

Company B will eventually hit a wall where they need outside capital.

The Acquisition Channel Framework {#channel-framework}

Not all acquisition channels are equal. Some are:

  • High CAC, high LTV (Enterprise sales)
  • Low CAC, high LTV (Viral growth)
  • Medium CAC, medium LTV (Content marketing)
  • Low CAC, low LTV (Paid ads with low-value customers)

Evaluating Channels: The Decision Matrix

Original Framework: Five-Point Channel Evaluation

For each potential channel, score on these dimensions:

| Dimension | What It Means |

|-----------|---------------| | Reach | How many potential customers can you access? | | Cost | How much does it cost per acquisition attempt? | | Conversion | What % of attempts convert to customers? | | Repeatability | Can you do this consistently at scale? | | CAC Efficiency | Is the CAC sustainable relative to your LTV? | Example: Evaluating Channels for a B2B SaaS product

| Channel | Reach (1-10) | Cost (1-10) | Conversion | Repeat (1-10) | Score | Recommendation |

|---------|-------------|-----------|------------|---------------|-------|-----------------| | Direct Sales | 7 | 8 | 5-10% | 8 | 7 | START HERE | | Content Marketing | 9 | 2 | 1-3% | 10 | 8 | BUILD NOW | | Paid Search | 8 | 7 | 2-5% | 9 | 7 | TEST SMALL | | Partnerships | 6 | 4 | 5-15% | 7 | 6 | TEST | | Events | 5 | 8 | 3-8% | 5 | 5 | SKIP | | Cold Email | 9 | 1 | 1-3% | 8 | 7 | BUILD NOW | What to look for:
  • High reach + low cost + high conversion = Ideal
  • High reach + high conversion + high cost = Scalable if profitable
  • Low reach + high cost + low conversion = Avoid

Channel Playbook: Build One Channel at a Time

Myth: You should try all channels in parallel. Reality: Master one, then add a second.

Here's why:

1. Each channel requires different skills and systems 2. Spreading resources thin means you're bad at all of them 3. Mastering one gives you data to evaluate others Your channel playbook: Months 1-3: Find Your Primary Channel
  • Pick your highest-scoring channel from the matrix
  • Allocate 80% of acquisition budget
  • Learn it deeply
  • Optimize it relentlessly
Months 4-6: Optimize Primary Channel
  • Get CAC to your target
  • Prove it's repeatable at scale
  • Document the playbook
Months 7-9: Add Secondary Channel
  • Pick your second-highest-scoring channel
  • Allocate 20% of budget
  • Same optimization cycle
Months 10+: Multi-Channel
  • Primary: 60% of budget (optimized, proven)
  • Secondary: 30% of budget (growing)
  • Testing: 10% of budget (new channels)

Building Your Acquisition Engine {#engine}

An acquisition engine is a repeatable, scalable system for turning leads into customers.

It has four stages:

Stage 1: Attraction (Get attention)
  • How do people discover you? (Search, ads, content, word of mouth)
  • What's your competitive advantage in getting found?
Stage 2: Engagement (Start conversation)
  • How do you get them to care? (Demo, trial, content, conversation)
  • What's your hook? (Why should they spend time with you?)
Stage 3: Conversion (Move to customer)
  • How do you close? (Sales call, self-serve, hybrid)
  • What's your conversion rate? (Depends on process)
Stage 4: Onboarding (Successful activation)
  • How do you ensure they succeed? (Onboarding, training, support)
  • What % actually activate and stick around?

Building Your Engine: The Funnel

Your CAC is: Acquisition Spend / 1.8 customers

But notice: Every stage has a funnel loss. Your job is to optimize each.

Optimizing Each Stage

Optimize Attraction:
  • Measure: How many people discover you?
  • Improve: Get more visibility (content, ads, partnerships)
  • Target: For every $1 spent, get X leads
Optimize Engagement:
  • Measure: Of 100 people who see you, how many engage? (20% in example)
  • Improve: Better messaging, clearer value prop, easier entry point
  • Target: 25-30% of visitors engage
Optimize Conversion:
  • Measure: Of engaged people, how many buy? (10% in example)
  • Improve: Better demo, clearer pricing, faster sales cycle
  • Target: 15-25% of engaged people convert
Optimize Onboarding:
  • Measure: Of customers, how many activate? (90% in example)
  • Improve: Better onboarding, clearer success path
  • Target: 95%+ activate

The Conversion Rate Playbook

Your conversion rate is the product of all these stages:

Overall Conversion = Engagement % × Conversion % × Activation %

Example:

  • Engagement: 25%
  • Conversion: 20%
  • Activation: 95%
  • Overall: 25% × 20% × 95% = 4.75% of visitors become active customers

If you get 10,000 visitors/month:

  • 10,000 × 4.75% = 475 customers/month

If your CAC target is $1,000:

  • You can spend $475,000/month on acquisition (!)

Each 1% improvement in any stage compounds:

  • Engagement 25% → 30%: 5.95% overall (25% improvement)
  • Conversion 20% → 22%: 5.18% overall (9% improvement)
  • Activation 95% → 97%: 4.88% overall (3% improvement)
Focus on the biggest lever first.

  • New conversion: 110 customers/month

With the same budget, you're acquiring 2.2x more customers at 55% CAC reduction.

Multi-Channel Strategy {#multi-channel}

Once you've mastered one channel, add others.

Channel Architecture for High Growth

Tier 1: Core Channel (60% of spend)
  • Your proven, optimized channel
  • Lowest CAC, highest volume
  • Example: Content marketing, direct sales, product-led growth
Tier 2: Growth Channel (30% of spend)
  • Secondary channel with good metrics
  • Higher CAC or lower volume than Tier 1, but still efficient
  • Example: Paid ads, partnerships, cold outreach
Tier 3: Experimental (10% of spend)
  • New channels you're testing
  • High risk, high reward
  • Example: Influencer partnerships, new ad networks, affiliates

Example: Multi-Channel for SaaS

Tier 1: Content + SEO (60%)
  • 40% of customers from organic search and content
  • CAC: $400
  • LTV: $5,000
  • Ratio: 1:12.5
Tier 2: Paid Search + Paid Social (30%)
  • 30% of customers from ads
  • CAC: $800
  • LTV: $5,000
  • Ratio: 1:6.25
Tier 3: Partnerships + Affiliates (10%)
  • 10% of customers from partners
  • CAC: $200
  • LTV: $5,000
  • Ratio: 1:25
Rest: Direct + Word of Mouth (20%)
  • Enterprise sales + referrals
  • CAC: $2,000
  • LTV: $20,000
  • Ratio: 1:10
Overall: Blended CAC of ~$700, LTV of $7,000, ratio 1:10

Case Studies: Real Acquisition Plays {#case-studies}

Case Study 1: From Paid Ads to Content

Company: B2B SaaS, Series A, $1.5M ARR The Problem:
  • Growth had plateaued at $1.5M
  • All customers came from paid ads
  • CAC: $3,000
  • LTV: $8,000 (ratio 1:2.67)
  • Payback period: 7 months
  • Burning cash to grow; couldn't scale fast
Why it was a problem:

The payback period was too long. Every new customer meant 7 months before they paid for themselves. To grow, they'd need to raise capital.

The Diagnosis:

They looked at their paid ad data:

  • Paid search: CPC $2, 2% conversion = CAC $100 per lead, but only 5% of leads convert to customers = $2,000 CAC
  • Display ads: CPC $0.50, 0.5% conversion = CAC $100 per lead, only 3% convert = $3,300 CAC
  • Paid social: CPC $1, 1% conversion = CAC $100 per lead, only 4% convert = $2,500 CAC

All three channels had the same problem: Low conversion from lead to customer. The unit economics only worked if you could get a 20%+ conversion rate, but they were at 3-5%.

The Decision:

Instead of trying to improve paid ad conversion (which is hard for an unknown brand), shift investment to content marketing.

The Play:

1. Hire a content/SEO person (Month 1)

2. Create 10 long-form guides addressing customer pain points (Month 1-2) 3. Optimize for search (Month 2-3) 4. Build email nurture sequence (Month 3-4) The Economics:
  • Month 1-3: Slow (4-5 customers/month from content)
  • Month 4-6: Increasing (10-12 customers/month, CAC dropping)
  • Month 7-9: Significant (20+ customers/month)
  • Month 10-12: Strong (30+ customers/month, CAC $500)
Year 2 result:
  • Revenue: $3.2M (2.1x growth)
  • CAC: $500 (from $3,000, 6.x improvement)
  • Payback: 3 months (from 7 months)
  • Can now scale without raising capital
The lesson: Poor CAC unit economics force you to choose: raise capital or change your acquisition strategy.

Case Study 2: Multi-Channel Distribution

Company: PLG SaaS, $2M ARR, 5 people The Goal:

Get to $10M ARR while staying capital-efficient

The Challenge:
  • Current channel: Product-led growth (trial → free → paid)
  • CAC: $100 (very low)
  • LTV: $4,000
  • But growth was plateauing (market saturation)
The Multi-Channel Play: Channel 1: Content + SEO (Target: 40% of growth)
  • Hire: Content/SEO person
  • Strategy: Long-form guides, tutorials
  • Timeline: 6-9 months to see traction
  • Projected CAC: $200-300
Channel 2: Partnerships (Target: 30% of growth)
  • Strategy: Partner with complementary tools
  • Approach: Revenue-sharing model
  • Timeline: 2-3 months to sign first partners
  • Projected CAC: $400 (higher, but higher deal value)
Channel 3: Sales (Target: 20% of growth)
  • Hire: 1 sales rep
  • Strategy: Target high-value customers directly
  • Timeline: Immediate (can start Day 1)
  • Projected CAC: $1,500 (high, but LTV is $10K+ for enterprise)
Channel 4: Keep PLG (Target: 10% of growth)
  • Optimization: Improve conversion funnel
  • Strategy: A/B test pricing, onboarding, upsell
  • Timeline: Ongoing
  • Projected CAC: $75 (improve from $100)
Timeline:
  • Month 1-3: Launch sales, optimize PLG, start content
  • Month 4-6: Add partnerships, refine all channels
  • Month 7-12: Scale what works, kill what doesn't
Result (year 2):
  • 40% of growth from content ($4M ARR, CAC $250)
  • 30% from partnerships ($3M ARR, CAC $400)
  • 20% from sales ($2M ARR, CAC $1,500)
  • 10% from PLG ($1M ARR, CAC $75)
Blended CAC: $530 Overall LTV: $5,200 (mix of customer types) Ratio: 1:9.8

This is sustainable and scalable. Each dollar of acquisition generates almost $10 in lifetime value.

Conclusion {#conclusion}

Acquisition is a numbers game.

The best operators:

1. Know their LTV and CAC precisely 2. Master one channel before adding others 3. Continuously optimize conversion funnels 4. Build sustainable, repeatable systems

Do this, and you can grow to $10M+ ARR without external capital. Skip this, and you'll be dependent on funding rounds.

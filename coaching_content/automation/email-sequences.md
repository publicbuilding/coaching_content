**Send:** Tuesdays, 8:00 AM (follows Email 2 after 4 weeks)  
**Subject Line:** The one hire that derails growing companies  
**From:** [Your Name] <[email@yourdomain.com]>  
**Reply-To:** [email@yourdomain.com]  
**Target Metrics:** 42% open rate, 19% click rate

---

[First Name],

Let's talk about the hire that destroys fast-growing companies.

It's not usually the incompetent person. You can fix that in 90 days.

It's the credible-sounding person who looks good but slows you down.

Usually it's someone senior. Someone with a great resume. Someone who's run things before.

But they got to the interview because you needed the role filled, not because they were the right fit for *this* stage.

**What happens:**

You hire them because they look credible. They spend the first 60 days "ramping." Then they spend the next 60 days proposing major changes based on lessons from their last job.

You like the changes. They seem smart. So you implement them.

Suddenly you've got 3 new systems, 2 new processes, and everyone's confused.

The person looks productive. But they've actually slowed the whole company down.

**Here's what I've learned:**

For fast-growing companies, hiring is not about hiring the smartest person. It's about hiring people who:
1. Can operate in ambiguity
2. Don't need to re-prove their seniority
3. Will slow down long enough to understand how things work before changing them
4. Actually want to build this specific thing, not use it as a resume-builder

**The test:** If someone in an interview talks more about what they've already done than about what they want to build next, be careful.

They're looking backward. You need people looking forward.

**Your move:** If you're hiring, ask three questions:
1. "Tell me about a time you had to slow down before moving fast."
2. "What's one system you implemented at your last job that you'd do differently now?"
3. "Why do you want *this* job, not just a senior role somewhere?"

Their answers will tell you everything.

### Email 4: The Communication Breakdown That Tanks Culture at Scale
**Send:** Tuesdays, 8:00 AM (follows Email 3 after 4 weeks, then repeats)  
**Subject Line:** How communication breaks down (and how to fix it before it kills you)  
**From:** [Your Name] <[email@yourdomain.com]>  
**Reply-To:** [email@yourdomain.com]  
**Target Metrics:** 44% open rate, 20% click rate

---

[First Name],

I was talking to a founder yesterday.

Series A company, 18 people. Everything was going well until about 3 months ago.

Suddenly: Misaligned decisions. People asking "why didn't I know about this?" Frustration. Drama.

Nothing was actually broken. But everything *felt* broken.

**What happened?** Communication systems didn't scale.

When you're 4 people, communication is easy. You're in the same room. Someone says something, everyone hears it.

At 8 people, you need more structure. A weekly meeting, maybe.

At 15+ people, you need a *lot* more structure. And most founders don't build it.

**Instead, they just talk louder.**

They send more messages. They repeat themselves. But it doesn't land because there's no system.

**Here's what actually works:**

**One: Synchronous meetings with structure**
- Weekly all-hands with an agenda (sent 24h in advance)
- Bi-weekly team meetings with rotating leadership
- Clear: Who updates, who decides, who needs to know

**Two: Asynchronous clarity**
- Written decisions stored somewhere everyone can find them
- Weekly summary emails so people know what happened
- Explicit: "This decision was made because..."

**Three: One source of truth**
- Not Slack. Not email. One system for important info.
- People know where to find it.
- You update it religiously.

**The test:** Next week, ask three people: "What's the company's top priority right now?"

If you get three different answers, you've got a communication problem.

Fix it now. It only gets worse as you scale.

## What Are OKRs?

OKRs = Objectives + Key Results

**Objective:** What direction are we going? (Qualitative)
**Key Results:** How will we know we got there? (Quantitative)

### Example OKR:

**Objective:** Become the most trusted payment platform for startups

**Key Results:**
1. Increase NPS from 45 to 65
2. Grow revenue 50% YoY
3. Reduce churn from 5% to 2% monthly

## OKR Benefits

1. **Alignment:** Everyone knows company priorities
2. **Clarity:** No ambiguity about what matters
3. **Focus:** Say no to non-priority work
4. **Accountability:** Track progress, adjust

## Setting OKRs

### The Cadence

**Company OKRs:** Set quarterly (3-month cycles)
**Team OKRs:** Derived from company OKRs
**Individual OKRs:** Support team OKRs

### The OKR Setting Process

**Week 1:** Leadership proposes company OKRs
**Week 2:** Teams propose OKRs (aligned to company)
**Week 3:** Alignment and refinement
**Week 4:** Finalize and publish

### Good OKR Characteristics

- **Ambitious:** 70% confidence you'll achieve it
- **Measurable:** Clear success criteria
- **Actionable:** Team controls the outcome
- **Aligned:** Supports company strategy

### Bad OKRs

- "Improve quality" (not measurable)
- "Grow revenue 500%" (unrealistic)
- "Hire 50 people" (not company goal)

## Tracking OKRs

### The Weekly Check-In

Every week, assess progress:
- Green: On track
- Yellow: Slightly behind
- Red: Off track, needs intervention

Track percentage completion (0-100%).

### The Mid-Quarter Check-In

At week 6, assess overall progress. Make adjustments if needed (pivot or drop if circumstances changed).

### The End-of-Quarter Review

Assess final results:
- Achieved: ✓
- Partially achieved: ~
- Not achieved: ✗

Average achievement: 70% is good (means OKRs were ambitious).

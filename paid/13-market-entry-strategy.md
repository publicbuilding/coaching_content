## The Market Expansion Problem

You've dominated your home market. Growth is slowing. You look at adjacent markets and see opportunity.

But entering new markets is hard:
- Different customer needs (they might not want what you built)
- Different go-to-market (what worked for sales won't work here)
- Different economics (price tolerance might be lower)
- Different competition (different competitive landscape)
- Different regulations (new compliance requirements)

Most companies expand and fail. Why? Poor evaluation of market fit and go-to-market strategy.

### The Market Selection Framework

Not all adjacent markets are equal. Some are easier to enter and more profitable than others.

**Evaluate on three dimensions:**

1. **Market Size:** Is the market big enough? (Minimum $50M TAM for focused entry)
2. **Competition:** How crowded is the market? (Fewer competitors = easier)
3. **Fit:** How well does your product fit? (Perfect fit = faster entry)

**Matrix:**

```
Large TAM, Low Competition, High Fit = GREEN LIGHT (expand)
Large TAM, High Competition, Medium Fit = YELLOW (proceed with caution)
Small TAM, High Competition, Low Fit = RED (don't enter)
```

### Market Entry Strategies

**Strategy 1: Geographic Expansion**
- Same product, different geography (US to Europe)
- Pros: Same product, just new distribution
- Cons: Different regulations, languages, distribution
- Timeline: 12-24 months
- Risk: Medium

**Strategy 2: Product Expansion**
- New product for existing customers (add module)
- Pros: Existing relationships, known value
- Cons: New product = new team/expertise
- Timeline: 6-12 months
- Risk: Low-Medium

**Strategy 3: Customer Segment Expansion**
- Same product, different customer type (SMB to Enterprise)
- Pros: Known product, new buyer
- Cons: Different buying process, price sensitivity
- Timeline: 9-18 months
- Risk: Medium

**Strategy 4: Competitive Displacement**
- Enter market to displace competitor (cheaper, better, faster)
- Pros: Large market, proven need
- Cons: High competition, need clear advantage
- Timeline: 18-36 months
- Risk: High

Pick the strategy with best risk/reward fit.

### The Market Testing Framework

Before committing major capital, test the market.

**Test 1: Customer Interviews (Week 1)**
- Talk to 20 target customers
- Do they have the problem?
- Would they buy your solution?
- What's their willingness to pay?

**Test 2: Landing Page + Ads (Week 2-4)**
- Create landing page for the new market
- Run paid ads to get leads
- What's your CAC? Conversion rate?
- Validate demand

**Test 3: Soft Launch (Month 2-3)**
- Sell to 10-20 customers at discounted rate
- Learn what works/doesn't
- Measure churn (is it lower or higher than home market?)
- Iterate

**Success criteria for market entry:**
- CAC is acceptable (<6 months payback)
- Churn is similar to home market
- Customer is asking for it (inbound demand)

## Go-to-Market Strategy

How you enter the market is as important as the market itself.

### Channel Strategy

**Direct Sales (High touch)**
- Pros: Higher ACV, customization, relationship building
- Cons: Expensive ($150K+ to hire / $200K to run)
- Use when: ACV >$50K, complex sales

**Inside Sales (Medium touch)**
- Pros: Scalable, faster, decent economics
- Cons: Lower ACV than direct
- Use when: ACV $10-50K

**Self-Service (Low touch)**
- Pros: Highly scalable, low CAC
- Cons: Requires product fit, organic demand
- Use when: ACV <$10K, clear self-serve use case

**Partnerships (Indirect)**
- Pros: Leverage partner channels, low upfront cost
- Cons: Lower margins, partner alignment required
- Use when: Partner has existing customer base you can reach

### Messaging and Positioning

Different markets care about different things.

**Example:**
- SMB market: "Reduce costs" (price-sensitive)
- Enterprise market: "Reduce risk" (compliance-sensitive)
- Startup market: "Speed to market" (velocity-sensitive)

Message to each market's priorities.

## Case Studies

### Case Study 1: The Successful Geographic Expansion

**Situation:** SaaS company, $5M ARR, dominant in US market.

Expansion target: Europe (large TAM: $200M, growing faster than US)

**Evaluation:**
- TAM: Large ✓
- Competition: Less crowded than US ✓
- Fit: High (product solves real problems in Europe) ✓
- Risk: Medium (language, GDPR, distribution)

**Strategy:**
- Hire European GTM leader (understands market)
- Translate product + website
- Hire European sales team (build relationships)
- Adjust pricing for local economics
- Handle GDPR compliance

**Results (Year 1-2):**
- Europe revenue: $2M ARR (accelerating)
- CAC: Higher initially, but trending down
- Retention: Matching US (good sign)

## Anti-Patterns

### Anti-Pattern 1: Expanding Without Product-Market Fit

**Trap:** We have product-market fit in home market, so let's expand.

**Reality:** Different market = different fit. Can't assume it translates.

**Fix:** Validate fit in new market before major investment.

### Anti-Pattern 2: Ignoring Competitive Positioning

**Trap:** Our solution works at home; it should work everywhere.

**Reality:** Competitor might own the market with similar or better solution.

**Fix:** Analyze competitive landscape before entering.

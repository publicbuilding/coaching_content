## The Launch Framework

Launch success has three phases: Before, During, and After.

### Phase 1: Pre-Launch (6-8 Weeks Before)

**Week 1-2: Planning**
- Define success metrics (revenue target, customer count, media coverage)
- Identify launch channels (email, social, PR, partnerships)
- Create launch budget (marketing spend)
- Assign launch lead (owner of entire process)

**Week 3-4: Internal Preparation**
- Sales training (what to say to customers)
- Support training (they'll get questions)
- Create marketing materials (website, one-pager, video, case study)
- Document feature benefits (not just features)

**Week 5-6: Beta Testing**
- Early access to power users
- Get feedback loop going
- Iterate based on feedback
- Build case studies for launch

**Week 7-8: Final Prep**
- Finalize marketing materials
- Train support team (FAQ, common objections)
- Prepare email sequences
- Dry-run the launch

### Phase 2: Launch (Week of launch)

**Day 1: Go Time**
- Email announcement (existing customers, email list)
- Social media blitz (all channels)
- PR distribution (media)
- Direct outreach (key customers, partners)
- Blog post announcement

**Days 2-5: Momentum**
- Daily updates on social media
- Response to customer feedback
- Share early wins/testimonials
- Keep media coverage alive

**Week 2: Follow-up**
- Webinar for interested customers
- Case study stories
- Influencer spotlights
- Measure early adoption

### Phase 3: Post-Launch (Weeks 3-8)

**Measure Success**
- Compare to launch goals
- Analyze customer feedback
- Identify gaps/issues
- Plan improvements

**Iterate Based on Learning**
- What's working? Double down.
- What's not? Fix or kill.
- Customer feedback drives roadmap

**Sustain Momentum**
- Monthly launch updates
- Feature improvements
- Customer success stories
- Ongoing marketing

## Launch Channels

### Email (Highest ROI)

Send to:
- Existing customers (they upgrade, expand, refer)
- Email list (subscribers interested in your space)
- Partners (they refer customers)

Frequency:
- Launch announcement (day 0)
- "You might have missed" (day 3)
- "Early wins" (day 7)
- Weekly for month 1

### Social Media

- LinkedIn (B2B): Thought leadership, case studies
- Twitter: Fast updates, engagement, conversation
- TikTok/Instagram: B2C, creative, behind-the-scenes
- Slack communities: Direct audience, peer endorsement

### PR and Media

- Press release (TechCrunch, industry blogs, mainstream)
- Interviews (founder interviews, podcasts)
- Expert commentary (your team quoted in stories)

### Partnerships

- Partner announcements (mutual customers)
- Co-marketing (partner email their customers)
- Affiliates (partner earns commission)

### Direct Outreach

- Key customers (personal outreach from founder)
- Influencers (thought leaders in your space)
- Press (media coverage)

## Real Launch Examples

### Example 1: The "Soft Launch" That Became Big

**Situation:** SaaS product manager wanted to launch a new feature quietly, learn fast, then scale.

**Approach:**
- Shipped feature to 10% of customers randomly
- No announcement, no press
- Monitored adoption, feedback, metrics
- Found: 30% adoption rate, very positive feedback

**Results:**
- Week 1: Quiet rollout, gather feedback
- Week 2: Fix issues, improve onboarding
- Week 3: "Feature X is live" announcement
- Week 4: 60% of customers using feature

**Key:** Small launch → Learn → Then big launch

### Example 2: The Coordinated Launch

**Situation:** Company launching enterprise-focused product.

**Preparation (8 weeks):**
- PR firm engaged (4 weeks advance)
- Press releases sent (2 weeks advance)
- Customer success team trained (2 weeks advance)
- Influencers given early access (3 weeks advance)

**Launch day:**
- Press release distributed (6 am)
- Email to customers (7 am)
- Social media blitz (8 am)
- Press coverage starts (9 am onwards)
- Direct sales outreach (10 am)

**Results:**
- 50 press mentions in first week
- 300 sales inquiries in first 24 hours
- 50 customers in first month
- $1M ARR from product in first 12 months

## Anti-Patterns

### Anti-Pattern 1: Launch With No Prep

**Trap:** "Let's launch next week, figure it out later"

Result: No training, no marketing, no support, customers confused

**Fix:** 6-8 week prep minimum

### Anti-Pattern 2: Launch to Empty Room

**Trap:** Launch to existing customers only

Result: No new customers, no new revenue

**Fix:** Announce widely (email list, social, PR, partners)

### Anti-Pattern 3: Launch and Abandon

**Trap:** Launch and move to next product

Result: Feature plateaus, customers don't adopt, opportunity wasted

**Fix:** Support and optimize for 8+ weeks post-launch

## Launch Metrics

Track these to measure launch success:

- **Awareness:** How many people heard about it?
- **Adoption:** How many customers use it?
- **Satisfaction:** NPS/reviews (are customers happy?)
- **Revenue:** Did it generate revenue?
- **Referrals:** Are customers referring it?

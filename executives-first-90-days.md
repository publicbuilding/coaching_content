## I. Introduction: Why the First 90 Days Matter
**Length:** 0.8 pages | **Time:** 2 minutes

### The Window of Opportunity
- New executives have 90 days of "honeymoon period"
- After 90 days, impressions solidify and trust is either there or isn't
- Most executives waste this window on tactical firefighting
- Winners use it to establish leadership, build relationships, and set strategic direction

### The Cost of Getting It Wrong
- Bad first 90 days = 18-24 month recovery (if possible)
- Team loses confidence, resists change
- Stakeholders doubt competence before you've had a chance to prove it
- Strategic opportunities get missed while you're in reaction mode

### What You'll Get From This Playbook
1. **Week-by-week action plan** (what to do when)
2. **Stakeholder engagement framework** (who to talk to, how)
3. **Information gathering roadmap** (what to learn, how to learn it)
4. **Decision framework** (what to decide, by when)
5. **Communication strategy** (how to set tone, share vision)
6. **100-day assessment tool** (measure your success)

### Who This Is For
- New executives (new company, new role, promotion)
- Executives in new industries/markets
- Leaders stepping into chaos/turnaround situations
- Anyone feeling uncertain about the first 90 days

## II. The 90-Day Framework (Overview)
**Length:** 0.5 pages | **Time:** 1 minute

### The Three Phases

#### Phase 1: Learning (Days 1-30)
**Goal:** Understand the situation, build relationships, earn credibility
- Listen more than talk
- Ask questions, don't give answers
- Observe what's working and what's broken
- Connect with key stakeholders

#### Phase 2: Leading (Days 31-60)
**Goal:** Set direction, make key decisions, build confidence
- Share your perspective
- Make strategic decisions
- Set expectations
- Build your team and culture

#### Phase 3: Building Momentum (Days 61-90)
**Goal:** Execute, deliver results, establish track record
- Launch strategic initiatives
- Get early wins
- Build positive momentum
- Plan for next phase (90+ days)

### Success Metrics by Phase
| Phase | Primary Goal | Key Measure | Success Look Like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learning | Understanding | Relationships built | Stakeholders know and trust you |
| Leading | Credibility | Decisions made | Team aligned on direction |
| Building | Momentum | Results delivered | Early wins, team confidence |

## III. Phase 1: Learning (Days 1-30)
**Length:** 2.5 pages | **Time:** 5 minutes

### Week 1: Orientation & Listening

#### Days 1-3: Arrival & Setup
**Administrative**
- [ ] Office setup, systems access, payroll setup
- [ ] Meet HR, finance, ops leads (admins who run things)
- [ ] Learn company systems, structure, org chart
- [ ] Schedule meetings with direct reports (individually, not group)

**Listening**
- [ ] Ask direct reports: "What are you working on? How's it going? What do you need from me?"
- [ ] Don't propose changes, just listen
- [ ] Take notes on patterns (what's mentioned by multiple people?)

**Mindset**
- "I'm here to learn"
- "Help me understand"
- Questions > statements

**Outcome by Day 3**
- [ ] You've met all direct reports
- [ ] You understand their priorities
- [ ] People know you're interested in their perspective

#### Days 4-7: Stakeholder Listening Sessions
**Who to Meet**
- Direct reports (already done)
- Their peers (same level in org)
- Boss (your boss)
- Key customers (top 3-5)
- Key vendors/partners
- Board members/investors (if applicable)
- Cross-functional leaders (finance, legal, ops if you're not one)

**Listening Session Format** (30-45 min each)
```
1. Context: "I'm new, learning the business, want your perspective"
2. Questions:
   - "What's working well in our business/team?"
   - "What's biggest challenge right now?"
   - "What should I know that I might not hear from official channels?"
   - "What would success look like in my role?"
   - "What's your advice for me in first 90 days?"
3. Listen, don't defend or debate
4. Take notes
```

**Patterns to Watch For**
- What's mentioned by 3+ people? That's important.
- What's the energy level when they discuss different topics?
- What do people seem worried about?
- What's the "unsaid" thing in the room?

**Outcome by Day 7**
- [ ] You've met all key stakeholders
- [ ] You've heard their perspectives
- [ ] You see patterns in what matters
- [ ] People feel heard

### Week 2: Data & Context Gathering

**What to Learn**
- Financial health (revenue, profitability, unit economics)
- Org structure (roles, responsibilities, reporting)
- Strategy (where are we going, how do we win?)
- Culture (values, how decisions get made, what matters)
- Market (who are customers, who are competitors, what's changing)
- History (what happened before, why did previous initiatives succeed/fail)

**How to Learn It**
- Finance: Ask for last 3 years P&L, cash flow, key metrics
- Strategy: Ask for business plan, market analysis, competitive assessment
- Team: Ask for org chart, roles, performance ratings
- Culture: Attend all-hands, read internal communications, observe meetings
- Customers: Ask to sit in on calls, read customer feedback
- Previous initiatives: Ask "what happened with X project?" in stakeholder meetings

**What NOT to Do Yet**
- Don't criticize previous leadership
- Don't propose changes
- Don't take sides in conflicts
- Don't make promises about changes

**Outcome by Day 14**
- [ ] You understand financial situation
- [ ] You know strategic direction
- [ ] You've identified key challenges
- [ ] You've spotted potential quick wins
- [ ] People view you as learning, engaged, open

### Weeks 3-4: Deep Dives & Assessment

**Deep Dives (Pick Top 3-5 Areas)**
- Pick highest-impact areas based on what you've learned
- Spend dedicated time (4-8 hours) on each
- Talk to people doing the work, not just leaders
- Understand root causes (not surface symptoms)

**Example Deep Dives**
- "Why is customer churn 15% annually?" (talk to 5-10 customers who left, frontline team, support)
- "What's blocking the product roadmap?" (talk to product, engineering, customers on what they need)
- "Why is our sales cycle 6 months?" (talk to salespeople, customers, competitors)

**Informal Assessment Structure**
- Strengths: What's this organization/team doing really well?
- Challenges: What's the biggest gap between current and potential?
- Quick wins: What's 1-2 things we could fix in 30-60 days?
- Structural issues: What requires longer-term change?

**Outcome by Day 30**
- [ ] You understand the business deeply
- [ ] You've earned credibility through listening
- [ ] You've identified 3-5 key opportunities
- [ ] You're ready to lead

### Phase 1 Checklist
- [ ] Met all direct reports individually
- [ ] Had listening sessions with key stakeholders
- [ ] Reviewed financial statements
- [ ] Reviewed strategy/plan
- [ ] Understood org structure and roles
- [ ] Identified strengths and challenges
- [ ] Identified 3-5 quick wins
- [ ] Built relationships (people trust you)
- [ ] Have perspective on culture (what matters, what's broken)
- [ ] Are ready to move to Phase 2

## IV. Phase 2: Leading (Days 31-60)
**Length:** 2.5 pages | **Time:** 5 minutes

### Week 5: Share Perspective & Get Feedback

#### First Leadership Communication
**Goal:** Share what you've learned and your perspective without overstepping

**Format: Town Hall or All-Hands**
```
1. Thank them for helping you learn
2. Share 2-3 key observations: "What I've learned in my first month..."
3. Share your leadership philosophy: "How I like to work..."
4. Ask for input: "What should we be focused on?"
5. Outline next 60 days: "Here's what we're going to do..."
6. Reinforce values: "What matters most to me..."
```

**Key Messages to Establish**
- You've listened and respect them
- You see the challenges and opportunities
- You have a vision (but still learning)
- You want collaboration
- You're committed long-term

**Outcome by Day 35**
- [ ] Team knows your perspective
- [ ] Team feels heard
- [ ] Team has clarity on priorities
- [ ] Team knows you're open to input

### Week 6: Key Decisions & Direction Setting

**High-Priority Decisions to Make**
- Top 3 strategic focus areas for next 90 days
- Who stays in their role, who might move
- What to kill/stop (projects, processes, meetings)
- Quick wins you'll tackle
- How you want to operate (meeting cadence, communication, decision-making)

**How to Decide**
- Gather data (already done in Phase 1)
- Seek input from key stakeholders (don't be a dictator)
- Make call based on what's right, not what's popular
- Communicate decision + rationale

**Critical Decision: Your Team**
- Who do you want around you?
- Are there people in wrong roles?
- Are there people you need to remove?
- Are there external hires you need?

**Reality:** Making right team decisions is 80% of success. Spend time here.

**Outcome by Day 42**
- [ ] Top 3 strategic priorities are clear
- [ ] Key decisions made
- [ ] Team aligned on direction
- [ ] Team composition clarified

### Weeks 7-8: Set Expectations & Build Culture

**Define "How We Work"**
- Decision-making process (consensus, consultative, directive?)
- Communication norms (frequency, channels, content)
- Meeting discipline (which meetings matter, cadence)
- Accountability (who's responsible for what)
- Values in action (what behavior reflects our values)

**Set Performance Expectations**
- For each direct report: what does success look like?
- How will you measure it?
- What support do they need?
- When will you check in?

**Establish Your Rhythms**
- 1:1 meetings (weekly, 30 min, always scheduled)
- Team meetings (what and when?)
- All-hands (monthly? quarterly?)
- Decision-making cadence (how often, how much notice?)

**Outcome by Day 60**
- [ ] Team knows expectations
- [ ] Working rhythms established
- [ ] Culture shift started
- [ ] People feel clear, aligned, supported

### Phase 2 Checklist
- [ ] Shared your perspective with full team
- [ ] Made key strategic decisions
- [ ] Clarified team composition
- [ ] Set performance expectations
- [ ] Established working rhythms and norms
- [ ] Identified and kicked off quick wins
- [ ] Have credibility with team
- [ ] Direction is clear

## V. Phase 3: Building Momentum (Days 61-90)
**Length:** 2 pages | **Time:** 4 minutes

### Week 9: Execute Quick Wins

**Goal: Get a W**
After 60 days of listening and decisions, execute something visible that works.

**Characteristics of Good Quick Wins**
- Achievable in 30-45 days
- Meaningful (people notice it)
- Team effort (shows collaboration)
- Within your control (don't depend on other departments)
- Demonstrates your leadership approach

**Examples**
- Reduce cycle time on X process by 20%
- Fix customer issue that's been nagging for months
- Launch new feature customers have requested
- Improve communication (e.g., better weekly updates)
- Streamline a meeting that's been wasteful
- Increase sales velocity on one deal type

**How to Execute**
1. Pick quick win
2. Assign owner
3. Daily or weekly check-ins (stay engaged)
4. Remove blockers
5. Celebrate when done

**Outcome by Day 75**
- [ ] 1-2 quick wins launched and near completion
- [ ] Team sees you can execute
- [ ] Team confidence rising
- [ ] Momentum building

### Week 10: Communication & Culture Building

**Share Results**
- Communicate quick wins to full team
- Explain what worked and why
- Celebrate team effort
- Show the pattern (this is how we operate)

**Deepen Culture**
- Share stories that reinforce values
- Start recognizing behavior you want to see
- Address behavior that doesn't fit values
- Model the culture (not just talk about it)

**Outcome by Day 82**
- [ ] Team sees results
- [ ] Culture shift visible
- [ ] Team believes in direction

### Week 11: Planning for Next Phase (90+ Days)

**Assess Your 90-Day Performance**
- Did you accomplish what you said you would?
- Do you have credibility with team?
- Do you understand the business?
- Have you made good strategic decisions?

**Strategic Planning (Next 90 Days)**
- What are the top 3 priorities for next quarter?
- What resources do you need?
- What will success look like?
- Who needs to change roles?
- What new initiatives will you launch?

**Team Development**
- Which direct reports are high-potential?
- Who needs coaching in specific areas?
- Who should be given stretch assignments?
- Do you need external hires?

**Personal Development**
- What are your growth areas?
- What feedback are you getting?
- What do you need to learn?
- Who can you learn from?

**Outcome by Day 90**
- [ ] You've delivered on your 90-day commitments
- [ ] Team believes in you
- [ ] Direction is clear for next phase
- [ ] Team is energized (not exhausted)

### Phase 3 Checklist
- [ ] Executed and completed 1-2 quick wins
- [ ] Communicated results and celebrated
- [ ] Culture shift underway
- [ ] Assessed your performance
- [ ] Planned for next phase
- [ ] Team has momentum and confidence

## VII. Critical Success Factors (Do These or You'll Fail)
**Length:** 1 page | **Time:** 2 minutes

### #1: Listen First, Talk Second
- Most new executives talk too much, listen too little
- Early advice = sounds arrogant
- Listening = shows respect and builds trust
- **What to do:** Ask more questions than you answer in Phase 1

### #2: Understand Before You Change
- Changes based on incomplete information = mistakes
- Mistakes kill credibility
- **What to do:** Don't propose changes until Day 31+

### #3: Make Decisions Clearly
- Indecision = chaos
- Wishy-washy leadership = loss of trust
- **What to do:** Make clear decisions, explain rationale, stick with them (unless new data changes them)

### #4: Overcommunicate
- You think you've said something once, people haven't heard it
- Alignment requires repetition
- **What to do:** Communicate in multiple formats, multiple times (meetings, email, 1:1s, all-hands)

### #5: Get Some Wins Early
- After 60 days of "assessing," people need to see results
- Quick wins build momentum and confidence
- **What to do:** Execute ruthlessly on Phase 3 quick wins (don't let them slip)

### #6: Build Your Relationships
- You're only as good as your relationships
- Relationships are built through:
  - Listening
  - Following through (doing what you say)
  - Showing you care
  - Being accessible
  - Being authentic
- **What to do:** Schedule 1:1s, actually have them, follow up

### #7: Take Care of Yourself
- First 90 days are exhausting
- Burnout = poor decisions
- **What to do:** Sleep, exercise, maintain perspective, don't move your family on Day 1

## VIII. The Traps: What Goes Wrong (And How to Avoid)
**Length:** 1 page | **Time:** 2 minutes

### Trap #1: The Early Answer Trap
**What happens:** Someone asks your opinion, you answer confidently.
**Why it's bad:** You don't know the full context yet.
**Better response:** "That's an interesting question. Help me understand more about X first..."

### Trap #2: The Change Trap
**What happens:** You see something broken on Day 5, change it by Day 10.
**Why it's bad:** You didn't understand why it was done that way. Change destroys trust.
**Better approach:** "I noticed X. Help me understand why we do it that way. Then we can assess if change makes sense."

### Trap #3: The Honeymoon Trap
**What happens:** You're in honeymoon period, avoid making hard calls.
**Why it's bad:** Hard calls become harder later (people get entrenched).
**Better approach:** Make hard people decisions by Day 60.

### Trap #4: The Isolation Trap
**What happens:** You stay in your office, don't engage with team.
**Why it's bad:** You miss context, people feel excluded, rumors spread.
**Better approach:** Walk around, have conversations, be visible and accessible.

### Trap #5: The "They Did It Wrong" Trap
**What happens:** You criticize previous leadership/decisions.
**Why it's bad:** People feel defensive, don't trust you, stop trying.
**Better approach:** "That made sense at the time. Now here's what we're going to do differently."

### Trap #6: The Perfectionism Trap
**What happens:** You wait for perfect information before making decisions.
**Why it's bad:** Decision-making grinds to halt, team loses confidence.
**Better approach:** Make decision with 80% of information on Day 45, not 100% info on Day 75.

### Trap #7: The Over-Commitment Trap
**What happens:** You say yes to everything in Phase 1.
**Why it's bad:** Can't deliver on everything, lose credibility.
**Better approach:** Be selective about commitments, pick battles you can win.

## XI. Common Questions (FAQ)
**Length:** 0.5 pages | **Time:** 1 minute

**Q: What if I inherited a crisis?**
A: Listen first (2 weeks), then act. Crisis requires urgency, but not chaos. Get stakeholder input, make decisions, execute.

**Q: What if the previous leader was terrible?**
A: Don't say it. Focus on future, not past. "We're moving in a new direction."

**Q: How much change is too much in 90 days?**
A: 1-2 major strategic changes, 3-5 operational changes. More = whiplash. Less = slow.

**Q: What if people resist my direction?**
A: Listen to concerns. If valid, adjust. If not, be clear about why you're proceeding. Resistance often means lack of clarity, not disagreement.

**Q: Should I fire underperformers immediately?**
A: No. Give them a chance to succeed under your leadership (30 days). If no improvement, then decide.

**Q: How do I balance listening with showing leadership?**
A: Listen in Phase 1 (weeks 1-4), Lead in Phase 2 (weeks 5-8), Execute in Phase 3 (weeks 9-12).

## Appendix: Templates & Tools

### A1: Stakeholder Interview Questions
```
1. Tell me about yourself and your role
2. What's working well right now?
3. What's your biggest challenge?
4. What should I know that most people might not tell me?
5. What's your advice for me in my first 90 days?
6. Who else should I talk to?
```

### A2: Quick Win Assessment Tool
```
Quick Win Assessment:
- Can we achieve it in 30-45 days? (Y/N)
- Is it meaningful to the team? (Y/N)
- Is it within our control? (Y/N)
- Does it demonstrate our leadership approach? (Y/N)
- Can we measure success clearly? (Y/N)

Proceed if YES to 4+ questions
```

### A3: Decision-Making Framework
```
For each major decision:
1. What's the decision we need to make?
2. What's the impact (scale of importance)?
3. Who needs to be consulted?
4. What's the deadline?
5. What's the reversibility (easy to undo or permanent)?
6. What new information might change our decision?
7. When will we decide?
8. How will we communicate the decision?
```
